Quick Clean · Case Study 01

Commercial Laundry Growth
in India

Quick Clean, founded in 2011, was working to shift the perception of commercial laundry from a generic vendor service to a structured operating partner for hotels and hospitals. When I joined in 2024, the marketing challenge was to build ICP clarity, sharpen messaging, and create a multi-channel demand generation engine from scratch.

40%
MQL to SQL
Conversion Lift
B2B GTM Hospitality + Healthcare ICP & Segmentation 6-Channel Engine ₹5Cr Opportunity Value
Overview
Business Context
Quick Clean provides outsourced commercial laundry services to hotels and hospitals in India. The category is largely unorganized — most properties rely on in-house laundry or informal local vendors. The opportunity was to reposition Quick Clean as a professional, zero-capex operating partner.
Marketing Challenge
No structured ICP, segmentation, or qualification framework. Messaging was generic — not tailored to hospitality vs healthcare, or by stakeholder role. Enterprise deals involved multiple decision-makers with different pain points and buying criteria.
Aman's Role
Marketing & Communication Manager. Responsible for ICP definition, GTM strategy, messaging, campaign execution, event presence, outbound systems, and funnel qualification. Did not handle operations, pricing negotiations, or customer success.
Primary ICP
Hospitality: Hotels with 150+ rooms (greenfield and brownfield). Healthcare: Hospitals with 250+ beds. Messaging varied by segment and stakeholder role within each segment.
Channels Used
Event marketing (Hotelier India, GM Summit), LinkedIn outreach, cold email sequences, WhatsApp follow-ups, and segment-specific landing pages and website content.
Primary Outcome
40% improvement in MQL to SQL conversion rate. Generated 30–40 qualified leads per quarter tied to an estimated ₹5Cr opportunity value. Built a repeatable, structured qualification framework.
Breakdown

Challenge · Strategy · Impact

Growth Challenge
  • Commercial laundry was an under-structured category — perception needed to shift from "vendor" to "partner"
  • Hotels and hospitals had entirely different buying triggers, pain points, and stakeholder hierarchies
  • Enterprise deals involved multiple decision-makers across departments
  • Messaging had to vary across greenfield hotels, brownfield hotels, and hospital segments
  • No structured segmentation, ICP definition, or qualification framework existed
Strategy
  • Defined ICP by property type, stage, room/bed count, laundry volume, cost pressure, and hygiene requirements
  • Built stakeholder-specific messaging for Hotel Owners, GMs, CFOs, EHKs, Purchase, and Engineering teams
  • Created segment-specific outreach: greenfield, brownfield, healthcare
  • Executed event marketing at industry-specific forums with direct decision-maker access
  • Built cold email and LinkedIn outreach aligned with ICP qualification criteria
  • Used WhatsApp follow-ups and case-study-led nurturing for stakeholder re-engagement
Business Impact
  • 40% improvement in MQL to SQL conversion rate
  • 30–40 qualified leads per quarter tied to ~₹5Cr opportunity value
  • Built a repeatable qualification framework and multi-channel acquisition engine
  • Improved funnel quality — fewer unqualified conversations, more structured pipeline
  • Helped shift marketing from generic awareness to structured, segment-specific acquisition

Email Sequence Logic
Follow-Up Cadence for Senior Decision-Makers

UHNI and senior hospitality decision-makers cannot be followed up repeatedly like low-ticket leads. Cadence was controlled, context-led, and spaced to respect their seniority.

1
Intro + Problem Frame
Segment-specific opening tied to their exact operational challenge (capex, space, hygiene). No generic pitch.
2
Case Study or Proof
Relevant hotel or hospital reference. Let proof do the persuading — not pressure.
3
Value Reframe
Cost-per-kg comparison or capex savings angle, tailored to CFO/Owner profile.
4
Soft Close / Check-In
Light-touch follow-up with a direct ask — "Is this worth a 20-minute call?" Spaced 7–10 days from previous.
ICP Qualification Framework
Structured Lead Scoring Logic
Leads were qualified on a combination of property type, size, current laundry setup, pain signal, and stakeholder seniority. This prevented wasted sales conversations with under-qualified accounts.

High priority: 150+ room hotels with in-house laundry pain, or 250+ bed hospitals with hygiene compliance urgency.
Medium priority: Pre-opening greenfield hotels where laundry setup was still being decided.
Low priority: Properties with established contracts already in place, or those below minimum size threshold.

Overview
Business Context
Bertrand & Bernard operated Shopify-led e-commerce brands. The challenge was to improve the efficiency and quality of paid acquisition — not just spend more, but get better returns from existing and new budget by improving the full conversion journey.
Marketing Challenge
ROAS was below target. Ad creative, audience targeting, landing page quality, and checkout flow all needed improvement. CPA was too high relative to average order value. No structured A/B testing process existed.

CRO Framework

Conversion Optimisation Approach

1
Audit & Data Collection
Heatmaps, session recordings, and funnel drop-off analysis to identify exactly where users were leaving the conversion journey.
2
Hypothesis Formation
Each test built around a specific hypothesis: "If we change X, conversion will improve because Y." No random changes.
3
Test Execution
A/B tests run across headlines, creative formats, page layouts, CTAs, product image order, and checkout copy. One variable at a time.
4
Scale Winners
Winning variants scaled across campaigns. Losing variants cut early to protect ROAS. Findings fed back into the next test cycle.

Business from Scratch

Before GTM strategies and acquisition funnels, I founded Meal House — a B2B food services business serving industrial companies across Bawal, Dharuhera, and Bhiwadi. No playbook. Just figuring out pricing, vendor management, logistics, and how to earn repeat corporate clients.

B2B Operations Industrial Food Services Vendor Management Client Acquisition Founder Story
Overview
Business Context
Meal House was a B2B food services business targeting industrial companies across the Rajasthan-Haryana industrial belt. The segment had a clear gap: factory workers and office staff needed reliable, quality food but most options were informal and inconsistent.
The Challenge
No established playbook. Had to figure out pricing, vendor sourcing, logistics, and repeatable delivery from zero. Corporate clients demanded consistency, hygiene, and punctuality — margin for error was low.
Aman's Role
Founder. Responsible for business model design, client acquisition, vendor coordination, pricing, logistics, operations, and repeat service delivery — the full business cycle, end-to-end.
Target Market
Industrial companies, factories, and corporate campuses across Bawal, Dharuhera, and Bhiwadi with recurring food service requirements for employees and staff.
Operational Models
Model 01: Central Kitchen with own delivery logistics. Model 02: On-premise kitchen setup inside company premises for larger clients needing dedicated, in-house food service infrastructure.
Key Learning
Shaped deep understanding of customer pain, pricing, operations discipline, and retention — foundational thinking that now drives how I approach GTM, unit economics, and growth strategy.
Breakdown

Challenge · Execution · Learning

The Challenge
  • No established market maps or vendor networks to rely on
  • Corporate clients demanded hygiene, consistency, and punctuality
  • Pricing had to balance thin margins, vendor costs, and competitive rates
  • Logistics and delivery reliability were critical to client retention
  • Building trust with procurement and admin decision-makers took persistent effort
Execution
  • Sourced and qualified local vendors for central kitchen supply and quality control
  • Designed two operational models based on client size and requirements
  • Built client acquisition through direct outreach, referrals, and on-site demos
  • Managed full delivery logistics including timing, consistency, and issue resolution
  • Handled client retention through regular check-ins and service reliability
Business Learning
  • Customer pain is best understood by being close to operations, not just data
  • Pricing must account for real unit economics, not just competitive benchmarking
  • Retention is built through execution discipline, not just relationship management
  • Scaling requires systemisation before growth — you can't hire out of broken ops
  • This experience informs how I think about GTM, product-market fit, and sustainable growth
Coverage
